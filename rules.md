# Open Lab Challenge Rules

There is no direct battle between groups. Each group independently works on its drawn topic for 90 minutes, then completes a 20-minute cycle: presentation, open Q&A, and peer voting.

Two groups may receive the same topic, but they are not opponents. They independently solve the problem and the class can compare their approaches during peer voting.

## Workflow

1. Draw a topic. At most two groups may share one topic.
2. Work independently for 90 minutes: research, baseline, solution, and experiment.
3. Complete a 20-minute presentation cycle: five minutes presentation, ten minutes Q&A, and five minutes peer voting.
4. Repeat the cycle for all six groups.

## Showcase Format

The presentation is at most five slides and five minutes:

1. Pain: the problem in one sentence, the affected party, and a failure hypothesis.
2. Baseline: the current or simple approach and why it is insufficient.
3. Solution: architecture, research technique, prototype, or demo.
4. Evidence: primary metric, before/after result, robustness result, and failure case.
5. Decision: deploy, rework, or reject, with risks and next steps.

At the five-minute hard stop, the presentation ends. A demo counts only when it supports evidence; it cannot replace evaluation.

## Q&A and Debate

During the ten-minute open Q&A, reviewers should challenge assumptions and production fit:

- Is the baseline too weak?
- Is ground truth biased?
- Does the metric measure the claimed pain point?
- Is there data leakage?
- What happens at ten times the scale?
- What is the cost of false positives or false rejections?
- Does the method work in a new domain?
- What is the most dangerous failure case?

Critique the solution, not the people. Presenters may answer with evidence or acknowledge a limitation.
